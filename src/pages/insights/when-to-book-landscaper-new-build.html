When to book your landscaper on a new build (earlier than you think)

By the Estate Landscapers team · Published 28 August 2026

The best time to price landscaping is when the slab goes down — here’s what it saves you.

Why “after handover” costs more

Most owners call a landscaper after the builder leaves. By then the site fence is down, the crane access is gone, and every decision the builder made about levels and drainage is set in concrete — sometimes literally. Work that would have been a note on a plan becomes excavation around a finished house.

What’s cheap early and expensive late

Three things top the list. Levels: agreeing finished ground levels with the builder prevents the classic 300 mm surprise between slab and boundary that becomes a retaining wall nobody budgeted. Drainage: sleeves and stormwater connections are trivial while trenches are open. Footings: a future pergola, shed or deck needs footings that cost little while machinery is on site and much more once turf and paving are down.

The ideal timeline

Slab down: get the landscape quoted, even if work starts months later — the quote surfaces the level and drainage questions while they’re still cheap to answer. Frame to lock-up: confirm scope and lock a start window keyed to practical completion. Handover: landscaping starts on a known plan instead of a discovery mission. Where your OC requires landscape items complete — driveways and retaining commonly — the early start isn’t an optimisation, it’s the critical path.

If you’re already past handover

Nothing is ruined — it’s simply a site-visit conversation instead of a plan-reading one. Photos, levels and access decide the approach, and an itemised quote shows where the site’s history costs money and where it doesn’t.
