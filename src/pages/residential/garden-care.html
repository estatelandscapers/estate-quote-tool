The calendar

Twelve months, one job each.

Sydney-calibrated. Do the month’s job and skip the guilt about the rest.

  1. January

    Deep, early-morning watering; raise the mowing height so turf shades its own roots through the heat.

  2. February

    Watch new plantings through the humidity — fungal issues show now. Water at the base, not over the leaves.

  3. March

    The best planting month of the year: soil is warm, heat is fading. Feed turf for a strong autumn.

  4. April

    Aerate compacted lawn areas and top up mulch to 75 mm before growth slows.

  5. May

    Last mow a touch shorter, clean up leaf fall off turf, and prune summer-flowering shrubs once they finish.

  6. June

    Slow season — service tools, plan changes, and plant bare-rooted trees and roses while they sleep.

  7. July

    Prune deciduous trees and roses. Check drainage grates and pits before the August winds bring debris.

  8. August

    Weed now while they’re small — the spring flush starts here. Prepare beds with compost for September.

  9. September

    Feed everything: turf, gardens, natives with native-safe fertiliser. First proper mow of the season.

  10. October

    Peak growing month. Mow weekly, mulch anything bare, and get new turf down while establishment is easy.

  11. November

    Set irrigation for the heat ahead and deep-soak young trees so roots chase the water down.

  12. December

    Mulch check before the heatwaves, raise the mower again, and water early morning only.

The kit

Gear that earns its shed space.

The short list we’d buy for our own yards — no gadgets. All available at the major hardware and garden retailers.

  • Mower with catcher

    The single tool that decides how the lawn looks weekly. Match the deck to the lawn size.

  • Hose-end sprinkler & timer

    For yards without built-in irrigation, a timer turns good intentions into an actual watering schedule.

  • Quality secateurs & loppers

    Clean cuts heal; crushed cuts invite disease. Worth buying once, properly.

  • Soil wetting agent

    Sydney soils turn water-repellent in summer — a wetting agent each season keeps water going down, not off.

  • Turf fertiliser (and a native-safe one)

    One for the lawn in September and March; a low-phosphorus one for native beds.

  • Mulch, annually

    75 mm on beds is the cheapest water-saving and weed-suppressing product there is.
